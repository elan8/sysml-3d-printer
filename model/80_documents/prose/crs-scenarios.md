Six operational scenarios describe how the printer is used. Together they make up the use case *produce a functional part*, which prepares a print, prints it and takes the part off the bed.

| Scenario | What happens | What it has to achieve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare and start a print | The engineer loads the filament, picks the material profile and starts a print from a sliced file. | The nozzle and the bed are at temperature and the first layer is down within five minutes of pressing start. |
| Print a part | The printer moves the toolhead and extrudes plastic layer by layer. | The part is finished quickly, within tolerance and without ripples or blobs, and the neighbour barely notices the machine. |
| Change material | The engineer switches from one filament to another, for example PLA to ABS, which needs a hotter nozzle and bed. | The change takes less than ten minutes, including the heat-up. |
| Calibrate | The technician levels the bed, tunes the heater control and measures the resonance of the axes so that the firmware can compensate for it. | The printer is calibrated in under thirty minutes and holds the calibration for the next hundred hours. |
| Handle a fault | A thermistor comes loose, a heater cartridge fails, a motor skips steps or a power cable shorts. | The printer notices, switches the heaters off and stops, without a fire and without damage beyond the print. |
| Maintain the printer | The technician retensions the belts, cleans the rails and replaces the nozzle. | Routine maintenance takes under an hour a month. |
