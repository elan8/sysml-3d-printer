The lead candidate is **CoreXYClosedShaped**: a CoreXY on an aluminium extrusion frame with 6 mm belts, closed-loop stepper motors, a high-flow hotend (80 W), a 400 W bed, a 600 W supply and a 32-bit control board with input shaping, pressure advance and a model-based thermal runaway monitor. It meets every requirement the architecture decides. The decision record (ADR-001) gives the reasons; in short:

- **A bed-slinger cannot reach the resonance, whatever the electronics.** Its worst axis is the 1.6 kg bed and part on a single belt, so its first resonance is 38 Hz against 50 Hz (SYS-005), its footprint is 12 percent over the limit (SYS-017), and the belt stretches so much that the tracking limit allows only 4 m/s^2 (SYS-003, SYS-008). With every electronic improvement it still fails those four.
- **Input shaping is what meets the ringing limit.** Without it the CoreXY's residual vibration at 10,000 mm/s^2 is estimated at 51 um against 50 um (SYS-006); the shaper brings it to 6 um, and the simulation of one axis confirms that without a shaper the carriage does not settle within 20 um, and with one it does, even when the shaper is tuned 11 percent off.
- **A basic 8-bit board is out.** It limits the step rate to 30 kHz, which on a CoreXY diagonal is 265 mm/s (SYS-002), its runaway check takes 40 s against 10 s (SYS-015), and it holds the nozzle temperature only to 2 K (SYS-012).
- **The belt, not the motor, limits the acceleration.** The motors allow 59 to 104 m/s^2 for the shaped candidates and the ringing limit 78, but the tracking limit (SYS-008) allows 13 m/s^2 for the lead candidate: a margin of 34 percent over the 10 m/s^2 required. A stiffer belt is the way to more. In the simulation no drive loses the move, even at 120 m/s^2.
- **Open-loop steppers fail only the noise limit** (56 against 55 dB(A)) and save 80 EUR; closed-loop steppers also damp the axis, so that with a trapezoidal move and no shaper they settle in 40 ms where an open-loop stepper never does.
- **Servos and a steel frame are the most accurate but not worth it.** The error budget is 40 um against 71 um, but the bill of parts is 887 EUR against the 750 EUR limit (SYS-019), the servos' peak power overloads the 600 W supply by 2 percent (SYS-014), and on the same axis a servo does not do better than a closed-loop stepper.

The decision commits the project to a calibration step that tunes the shaper to the measured resonance, and to confirming the belt stiffness (1.26e5 N/m, an estimate), on which the acceleration margin rests.
